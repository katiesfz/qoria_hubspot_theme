Recognising the varying degrees of digital safety is crucial for schools.
Digital safety can be categorised into three levels:

Basic protection involves blocking harmful or inappropriate websites in their entirety, typically through a firewall or free/low-cost filtering tool.

For schools, this is the most straightforward and budget-friendly approach. While this provides a first line of defence, it does not address the full range of online risks that students face today. Firewalls can block known harmful websites, but new content is emerging every second and they generally lack the flexibility that schools need to make changes on the fly to their filtering requirements. There is a significant time lag between harmful content going live and the firewall's ability to recognise and block it - leaving children exposed to risk. It's not uncommon for children who come across inappropriate content to share it amongst their friends - exacerbating the risk.

Schools with a  Better level of protection, are able to see risks and block harmful content as they emerge. Real-time, content aware filtering, real-time alerts and human moderated digital monitoring combine also to give schools a deeper insight into risks and their causes enabling faster and more appropriate interventions and a decrease in the likelihood of escalation.

At the Better level, digital safety provision is no longer a tick-in-a-box exercise but becomes more embedded in the school culture. The scope of risk detection is much wider now and takes into account student searches, app usage, and online interactions as well as deeper visibility into supposedly safe platforms like YouTube, Google Docs, email and Microsoft Teams.

The Best provision sees the responsibiity for digital safety shared across the school community, including IT teams, teachers, and parents. It ensures that students are protected on any network, on any device, at any time, at school and at home - as well as supporting teachers in managing distractions in the classroom. Best provision sees a level of protection far more aligned to the digital risks we see today.

Implementing this level of digital safety requires a whole-school approach that integrates advanced filtering, real-time monitoring, and classroom management tools. IT teams deploy and maintain a unified platform, while teachers gain the tools needed to manage student device use and respond to risks as they arise. Beyond the classroom, parents are provided with similar tools to ensure consistent digital safety at home. This approach requires investment in technology, training, and clear policies to ensure its effectiveness.
